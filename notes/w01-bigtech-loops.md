# Big Tech Hiring Processes: Application to Offer

A side-by-side summary of the public hiring guidance from **Google**, **Meta**, **Amazon** and **Microsoft**.

> **How to read this document**

> - **Not stated** means the page does not say. It does not mean the practice never happens.
> - **Not covered** means the supplied text did not include that topic at all.
> - Processes vary by role, level, location and team. Always check the job posting and your recruiter's instructions.

## Google

**Source:** Google Careers, "Our hiring process"
**Typical timeline:** roughly **6-8 weeks** (varies by role and team)

### Stages from application to offer

| # | Stage | What happens |
|---|-------|--------------|
| 1 | **Resume** | Tailor a resume to each job. Show alignment with the minimum qualifications (MQs), use data, and keep it short. Cover letters are not required. See [Create a Careers profile](https://www.google.com/about/careers/applications/profile). |
| 2 | **Research** | Learn about Google: [about.google](https://about.google/), [team sites](https://careers.google.com/teams/), [locations](https://careers.google.com/locations/), [benefits](https://careers.google.com/benefits/). |
| 3 | **Apply online** | Limit of **3 applications every 30 days**. [Explore open roles](https://www.google.com/about/careers/applications/jobs) and [set up job alerts](https://www.google.com/about/careers/applications/jobs/alerts?q=project%20manager). |
| 4 | **Assessments** *(may apply)* | Google Hiring Assessment (workstyle skills) and possibly role-related assessments such as a coding exercise. |
| 5 | **Recruiter conversations** | One or two shorter phone or video calls covering key skills, team info, and your preferences (e.g., location). |
| 6 | **Project work** *(role-dependent)* | Case study, writing sample, or code sample before interviews. |
| 7 | **Interviews** | A panel of interviews, by video or in person, with a rotating cast of Googlers. Structured rubrics and open-ended questions. No brainteasers. |
| 8 | **Decision** | Perspectives from the application and interviews are brought together for review. |
| 9 | **Offer** | The recruiter presents the offer, compensation structure and [benefits](https://www.google.com/about/careers/applications/benefits). [Global mobility support](https://www.google.com/about/careers/applications/benefits/mobility) is available for other locations. |
| 10 | **Onboarding** | Badging, insurance, etc., then you become a "Noogler". |

### What the page says about interviews

- Interviews are "a panel of interviews, either over video or in person." The number of rounds is not given.
- Every candidate is assessed with the same clear rubrics for the role (structured interviewing).
- Questions are role-related and open-ended, focused on problem solving, teamwork and strengths.
- Accommodations (sign language interpreters, specialized equipment, breaks, extended time) are available: [Accommodations at Google](https://www.google.com/about/careers/applications/stories/accommodations-at-google/).
- Prep: [How to prepare for interviews](https://careers.google.com/interview-tips/).

### Not covered in the supplied text

Onsite rounds by type and count, who makes the final decision, team matching, and AI tool rules.

### Stages from application to offer

|---|-------|--------------|

| 2 | **Apply** | Search the careers site and confirm you meet all minimum qualifications, with your resume clearly showing it. |

| 6 | **Onboarding** | If you accept, your up-to-date onboarding checklist appears in your career profile. |

## Amazon

**Source:** Amazon.jobs "How we hire" pages (online application, assessments, phone screening, interview loop)
**Typical timeline:** **not stated** overall. Response times are given for some steps.

> The process differs from role to role.

### Stages from application to offer

| # | Stage | What happens |
|---|-------|--------------|
| 1 | **Online application** | [Search jobs](https://www.amazon.jobs/search) and click "Apply Now." Create or log into a profile. Details: [Online application](https://amazon.jobs/content/en/how-we-hire/online-application). No cover letters are accepted. A resume is recommended, but if you have none you must give a brief description of your background, experience (with dates), education and skills. Warehouse and fulfillment center roles need no resume: [fulfillment center hiring process](https://hiring.amazon.com/hiring-process). |
| 2 | **Assessments** *(role-dependent)* | Online, usually remote. **Work style assessments** (10-20 min) are based on the Leadership Principles. **Work sample simulations** (20 min to 1 hr) are virtual job-related tasks. Some have deadlines with no extensions. Must be completed solely by the applicant. Details: [Assessments](https://amazon.jobs/content/en/how-we-hire/assessments). |
| 3 | **Phone screening** *(certain roles)* | Behavioral questions guided by the [Leadership Principles](https://amazon.jobs/content/en/our-workplace/leadership-principles), over Zoom or Amazon Chime. Some teams add role-specific exercises or online assessments. The recruiter tries to reply within **two business days**. Details: [Phone screening](https://amazon.jobs/content/en/how-we-hire/phone-screening). |
| 4 | **Interview loop** | Individual meetings with current employees, remote or in person. Details: [Interview loop](https://amazon.jobs/content/en/how-we-hire/interview-loop). |
| 5 | **After the loop** | A feedback survey arrives by email. Expect to hear back within **five business days**. Travel expense instructions follow if you traveled. |
| 6 | **Offer** | **Not stated** on the pages provided. |

Check your application status by logging into your application profile. Amazon says it cannot contact everyone who applies.

### Interview loop by type and count

The loop is a series of one-on-one interviews of about **45 minutes to 1 hour each**, with **two to seven interviewers**. The pages do not say how many rounds of each type there are.

| Type | Count |
|------|-------|
| Coding | **Not stated.** Technical prep applies to tech roles (e.g., software development, machine learning): [Software development topics](https://amazon.jobs/p-software-development-topics). |
| Design | **Not stated** |
| Behavioral | **Not stated** as a number. Interviews focus on behavioral questions tied to the Leadership Principles (STAR-style stories), with no brain teasers. |
| Other | **Not stated** as a number. Some roles ask for a **writing sample** (narrative memo, 1-6 pages) or a role-specific exercise, and the candidate is told in advance. |

Interviewers take detailed notes to share with the rest of the team, but the pages do not describe how the final decision is made.

### SDE II interview prep

Amazon says the SDE interview is designed to identify candidates with the technical proficiency, behavioral skills and cultural fit needed for its customer-centric mission. The online assessment includes a **coding challenge** and a **system design challenge**.

**Coding challenge**

- Try the sample coding challenge first to get comfortable with the environment.
- The timer is **90 minutes**. Once it starts, it doesn't stop, even if you exit. Check it regularly.
- Efficiency and optimization earn more points than brute-force solutions.
- Your code must compile for **all** questions to move forward in the process. Test it before you submit or before time runs out.
- You can compile as many times as you like, but there must be a **15-second interval** between consecutive compilations.
- Consider all edge cases and handle large inputs effectively.
- Supported languages: C, C++, C++14, C#, Go, Java7, Java8, JavaScript, Kotlin, Objective-C, PyPy2, PyPy3, Python2, Python3, Ruby, Scala, Swift.

**System design challenge**

- You apply your system design skills to a few fictional situations with simulated peers, managers and stakeholders.
- There is **no whiteboarding or diagram exercise**. You rate the most effective or ineffective actions to take in each scenario.
- Relevant concepts: designing for maintainability, determining trade-offs, and dealing with ambiguity.

**Not covered** in the supplied text, beyond the note above that the pages do not describe how the final decision is made.

### AI tools in interviews

**Candidate use of AI tools: not stated** on the pages provided.

## Microsoft

### Stages from application to offer

| # | Stage | What happens | Notes / official resources |

### Interview rounds by type and count

Most interviews involve **2-4 conversations**, each up to one hour. No standard breakdown by interview type is published, and the number may vary by role.

| Interview type | Published count | What can be concluded |
|----------------|-----------------|-----------------------|
| Coding / technical | **Not stated** | Some roles may require candidates to write code, but the overview does not say how many coding rounds occur. |
| System / product design | **Not stated** | No standard design-round count is given. |
| Behavioral / experience-based | **Not stated** | Candidates are advised to give examples from past experience, explain how they would approach role tasks, and connect their skills to the position. The number of behavioral rounds is not specified. |
| Portfolio / work samples / other | **Not stated** | Some roles may request a creative portfolio or work samples in other formats. The number of rounds or assessments is not specified. |
| **Total conversations** | **Usually 2-4** | Microsoft's stated general pattern for most interviews, not a guaranteed onsite-only count. |

### Team matching: before or after the offer?

### AI tools in interviews

The supplied text describes AI-powered job recommendations based on a resume or profile. It does not state a rule allowing or prohibiting candidates from using AI tools during interviews.

| Topic | Status |

**Recommended action:** follow the instructions for the specific assessment and ask the recruiter if the rules are unclear. Do not assume that AI used for job recommendations means candidates may use AI during interviews.
